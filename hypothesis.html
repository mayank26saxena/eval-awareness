Research hypothesis · revised

Reading the Mind or the Prompt?

We already know models often notice when they're being tested, because they sometimes say so. Newer tools that read activations report picking this up even when the model says nothing. Those reports haven't been checked against ground truth: the tools could be reading the model's internal state, or just noticing that the prompt looks like a test. This project runs that check.

The question: When an activation reader says "this model thinks it's being evaluated", is it picking up contextual cues the model actually processed, or just prompt format? And does the format-free part of that signal cause different behavior?

2. Three hypotheses

1

Readers follow context

NLAs, Activation Oracles and probes flag ① and ②, the prompts with test cues, even when ② looks casual. Text-only detectors get fooled by ② and ③.

2

A format-free signal exists

Remove the "looks like a benchmark" direction from the activations (LEACE). The context can still be decoded from what's left.

3

That signal drives behavior

Turning the format-free signal down makes the model more deceptive on a test it passed. Turning the format direction or a random direction down doesn't.

6. Either outcome is useful

If readers follow format

Eval-awareness readings, Anthropic's NLA results included, need format controls before anyone trusts them. Making evals look realistic is probably enough to fix the problem.

If readers follow context (and it's causal)

Rewriting won't fix eval awareness. Labs need evals built from production traffic plus internal monitoring. And we'd have a validated monitor to use.
