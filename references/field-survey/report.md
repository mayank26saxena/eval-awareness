# Interpretability pivots from decoding to verifying

As of October 2026, LLM interpretability has moved its center of gravity from "decompose a model into sparse features and trace circuits" toward "make internals usable for oversight": models that verbalize their own activations (Anthropic's Natural Language Autoencoders, Activation Oracles), cheap probes and steering judged against simple baselines (Google DeepMind's "pragmatic interpretability"), and interpretability tools handed to auditing agents. The field's hardest open problem is no longer finding features but **validating that explanations are faithful and causally meaningful**: Anthropic's own flagship 2026 method confabulates specifics, SAEs still fail to beat linear probes on downstream tasks, and 2026 papers disagree on whether evaluation-awareness directions are causal. Academically, mechanistic interpretability is booming at workshops (about **350 accepted papers at the ICML 2026 MI Workshop**, 600+ attendees at NeurIPS 2025's), but it won no main-track best-paper award at NeurIPS 2025, ICLR 2026 or ICML 2026. Its strongest conference showing was a cluster of ICLR 2026 orals on temporal SAEs, attribution-graph verification of chain-of-thought, and causal-intervention critiques. This shift favors independent researchers: the agenda-setters now explicitly want proxy-task-validated work on open models of 8B parameters or fewer, and several of 2025–26's most influential results (emergent-misalignment organisms, crosscoder fixes, Thought Anchors) came from MATS-scale projects on 0.5B–14B models. The ranked list at the end identifies ten problems where a Mac plus $500–2k of GPU can produce a publishable partial result. The top three are eval-awareness causality, faithfulness audits of released activation verbalizers, and cheap estimators of chain-of-thought step importance.

## Anthropic bet 2026 on models that explain their own activations

Anthropic's 2025 program was built on **attribution graphs over cross-layer transcoders**. "Circuit Tracing" and "On the Biology of a Large Language Model" (Mar 2025) mapped multi-hop reasoning, rhyme planning, multilingual circuits and unfaithful chain-of-thought (CoT) in Claude 3.5 Haiku, and July 2025 follow-ups extended the method to attention (QK) circuits, a new transcoder architecture (MOLT), and a toy model of how transcoders can be mechanistically unfaithful ([transformer-circuits.pub](https://transformer-circuits.pub/)). A secondary summary says circuit tracing gave satisfying insight on only about **a quarter of prompts tried** *[secondary source, not confirmed against the paper]* ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Olah)). The method was open-sourced as the `circuit-tracer` library in May 2025 *[background, not re-verified this pass]* ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/open-source-circuit-tracing)).

In 2026 the emphasis shifted to verbalization. The May 2026 **Natural Language Autoencoders (NLA)** paper trains an activation verbalizer (activation to text) and an activation reconstructor (text back to activation), both initialized from the target model and trained jointly with GRPO. They reach **0.6–0.8 fraction of variance explained**, a figure that grows with log training steps ([NLA](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/)). On Claude Opus 4.6, NLA explanations exposed rhyme planning: editing the explanation and steering switched "rabbit" to "mouse" about half the time. They also traced stray Russian outputs to malformed training data and surfaced unverbalized evaluation awareness, which was stronger on eval transcripts than on deployment traffic. NLA-equipped auditing agents beat SAE and black-box baselines at finding the root cause of a deliberately misaligned model ([NLA](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/)). Anthropic released training code, **trained NLAs for popular open models**, and a Neuronpedia frontend, which matters for independent researchers.

The paper's own limitations section reads like an open-problems list. Explanations are thematically faithful but **confabulate specifics**. NLAs have no mechanistic grounding. The verbalizer adds inferences not present in the activation. Training is expensive, requiring joint RL on two full LLMs. And degenerate or steganographic solutions are possible ([NLA](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/)). The July 2026 "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace" paper builds on this. It argues that Claude keeps a small set of representations it can report, control and reason with, layered over much larger automatic processing ([workspace](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/)). The surrounding work includes "Emergent Introspective Awareness" (Oct 2025), the Activation Oracles cross-post (Dec 2025) and "Emotion Concepts and their Function" in Claude Sonnet 4.5 (Apr 2026) ([index](https://transformer-circuits.pub/)). Anthropic has also kept weight-level work going on tiny models. The August 2026 paper "Characterizing interference weights in a tiny language model" decomposes a one-layer transformer's weights ([paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/interference_effectiveness_helpfulness/index.html)), which signals that transcoder "dark matter" and interference remain unresolved.

## DeepMind abandoned ambitious reverse-engineering for proxy tasks

Google DeepMind's mech interp team (Neel Nanda) made the field's most consequential agenda move. In March 2025 it deprioritized SAE research after SAEs **underperformed dense linear probes on out-of-distribution harmful-intent detection**; fine-tuning SAEs on chat data closed only about half the gap ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks)). In December 2025, "A Pragmatic Vision for Interpretability" made the turn formal. The team said it had "been disappointed by the amount of progress made by ambitious mech interp work" ([quote](https://x.com/morqon/status/1995544917259481216)). It now chooses problems on the critical path to AGI safety, measures progress on proxy tasks, and tries prompting, steering, probing and reading CoT before anything fancier ([Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/StENzDcD3kpfGJssR/a-pragmatic-vision-for-interpretability)). A companion post, "How Can Interpretability Researchers Help AGI Go Well?", is the best concrete list of open questions available. It calls reasoning-model interpretability "extremely neglected" and lists model diffing, steering what fine-tuning learns, probe-based monitoring, eval awareness, model organisms and latent reasoning ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MnkeepcGirnJn736j/how-can-interpretability-researchers-help-agi-go-well)).

GDM's 2026 output follows that agenda *(titles verified; year inferred from page order on the author profile)* ([AF profile](https://www.alignmentforum.org/users/neel-nanda-1)). It includes J-lens and R-lens readout methods (applied to Qwen3.6-27B), "Why do models task game?" as a misalignment proxy, "Does DiffusionGemma do latent reasoning?", "Astra can do a concerning amount with no chain of thought", and, in September 2026, **WorkspaceBench**: 3,356 questions testing whether activation-to-text tools can read intermediate variables. WorkspaceBench responds directly to Anthropic's global-workspace framing, a sign that the field is starting to benchmark verbalization tools rather than trust them. Nanda's February 2026 post defending research on training against interpretability signals ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/G9HdpyREaCbFJjKu5/it-is-reasonable-to-research-how-to-use-model-internals-in)) marks a live controversy. The open question is whether optimizing against probes or features destroys their value as monitors.

## OpenAI, Goodfire and Transluce stake out three other bets

OpenAI's main interpretability bet is **interpretable by design**. Its late-2025 weight-sparse transformers, in which each neuron has only a few dozen connections, yield disentangled circuits for simple behaviors that are about **16x smaller** than circuits pruned from dense models of comparable loss. The authors call it a "very ambitious bet", since these models are far below frontier scale ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/understanding-neural-networks-through-sparse-circuits/)). A secondary press report says a ~0.4B sparse model was open-sourced *[secondary, unverified]* ([36kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3596293967118594)). OpenAI's other levers are SAE "misaligned persona" features that control emergent misalignment ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/emergent-misalignment/)) and CoT monitoring, which beats action-only monitoring but is fragile under optimization pressure ([PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/34f2ada6-870f-4c26-9790-fd8def56387f/CoT_Monitoring.pdf)). A 2026 "Monitoring Monitorability" paper is described as an ICML 2026 spotlight *[secondhand, not confirmed on a primary page]*, and a LessWrong critique argues CoT-monitorability evaluation "is still an open problem" ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/z9fPtghFxELBXebY3/evaluating-chain-of-thought-monitorability-is-still-an-open)). The notes found no confirmed new 2026 OpenAI mech-interp paper.

Goodfire turned interpretability into a product layer. It raised a **$150M Series B at a $1.25B valuation** in February 2026 to build a "model design environment" ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-lab-goodfire-raises-150m-at-1-25b-valuation-to-design-models-with-interpretability-302680120.html)). It publishes "Features as Rewards", which uses interpretability features as RL rewards against hallucination; the company claims about a 50% reduction *[company claim, snippet-level only]* ([Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research/rlfr)). It also inherited the parameter-decomposition line from Apollo: APD, then SPD, then VPD. A May 2026 paper decomposed a full 67M-parameter, 4-layer LM ([Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research/interpreting-lm-parameters)). Lee Sharkey's framing is that no decomposition is objectively "right", because every method discretizes a continuous object ([Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/untangling-neural-network-mechanisms-goodfires-lee-sharkey-on-parameter-based-interpretability/)).

Transluce treats interpretability as **scalable oversight infrastructure**. Its work includes the open-source Docent transcript analyzer, trained explainer models, Predictive Concept Decoders, and "Scaling Activation Oracles to Trillion-Parameter Models" (Aug 2026), which finds that oracles improve with model size, data size and data quality ([Transluce](https://transluce.org/news)). Smaller streams continue elsewhere. Timaeus scaled singular-learning-theory "spectroscopy" to Pythia-1.4B ([Timaeus](https://timaeus.co/research/2026-04-21-spectroscopy-main)). EleutherAI works on automated attribution-graph interpretation and on how SAEs trained on the same data learn different features ([EleutherAI](https://eleuther.ai/papers/)). Across these labs, automated auditing agents are now standard. The September 2026 SAEScientist-Bench, run on Gemma Scope with Gemma-2-9B-IT, finds agents can discover features but show a **persistent gap in causal control** ([arXiv 2609.09113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113)).

The awards picture is lopsided. NeurIPS 2025's seven best papers and runners-up included no mechanistic-interpretability paper ([NeurIPS](https://blog.neurips.cc/2025/11/26/announcing-the-neurips-2025-best-paper-awards/)). One of ICLR 2026's two Outstanding Papers was an expressivity result ([Saarland](https://saarland-informatics-campus.de/en/piece-of-news/mpi-researcher-receives-outstanding-paper-award-at-iclr-2026/)). ICML 2026's Outstanding Papers went to diffusion and sampling work *[from secondary roundups that disagree on the announcement date]* ([ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/icml-seoul-roundup-diffusion-models-sweep-ai-research-awards)). Interpretability-adjacent work won where it framed itself causally. EMNLP 2025 Outstanding Papers went to measuring CoT faithfulness by unlearning reasoning steps (Belinkov group) ([Technion](https://www.cs.technion.ac.il/news/view-new.php?nwid=1581)) and to causal interventions revealing shared structure across filler-gap constructions (Potts group) ([CV](https://stanford.edu/~cgpotts/cv)).

The volume sits at workshops. The **ICML 2026 MI Workshop** (Seoul, Jul 10) listed about **350 accepted papers with 23 spotlights**, up from 140+ at ICML 2024. It was framed explicitly as a debate between "pragmatic and ambitious" approaches ([ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/54071)). Its invited talks preview where small-scale work is landing: unverbalized evaluation awareness, failures of final-token safety probes ("Before the Last Token"), dissociated sycophancy representations, steering of discrete diffusion LMs, and a unified mechanism for harmful content. The NeurIPS 2025 MI Workshop drew 600+ attendees, with keynotes from Olah, Been Kim and Schwettmann ([workshop](https://mechinterpworkshop.com/neurips2025/)). ## Faithfulness, not feature-finding, is the binding constraint

The negative results have shaped the frontier more than any positive method. On sparse probing across more than 100 datasets, SAE probes do not beat logistic regression ([arXiv 2502.16681](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16681v1)). An ICML 2026 paper argues that both linear probes and SAEs fail at compositional generalization ([ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/69389)). A Mila counterpoint blames a "brittle, amortised encoder" rather than superposition itself ([arXiv 2603.28744](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28744v1)). SAE features are unstable across seeds, although the subspaces they span are reproducible ([arXiv 2606.12138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12138)). Interpretability scores weakly predict how useful a feature is for steering ([arXiv 2510.03659](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03659v1)). The constructive response has two halves. One is better architectures: on the SynthSAEBench synthetic ground truth, Matryoshka BatchTopK is best, with probing F1 of about 0.88 ([arXiv 2602.14687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14687)). The other is a reframing: SAEs are for **discovering unknowns**, not acting on knowns ([arXiv 2506.23845](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23845)).

The same validation problem now shows up one level higher. NLAs confabulate details. Activation Oracles and decoders are black boxes explaining black boxes. Evaluation-awareness research shows the gap between correlation and causation sharply. Across 37 open models, probes keep **AUROC ≥0.98 even when rewrites push behavior below chance**, and single-layer steering has a "negligible" effect ([arXiv 2606.23583](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23583v1)). On Qwen3-32B the link between eval awareness and refusal is "correlative" and varies by prompt ([arXiv 2608.27340](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27340v1)). A third 2026 paper is titled "A Probe Direction Is a Property of Its Prompt"; its findings were not retrieved ([arXiv 2608.13329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13329)). Reasoning models compound the problem. Computation is spread across stochastic, non-differentiable token samples, so editing a CoT goes off-policy. Thought Anchors (R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) and Thought Branches (ICLR 2026) therefore rely on expensive on-policy resampling as ground truth ([arXiv 2506.19143](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19143v4); [ICLR slides](https://iclr.cc/media/iclr-2026/Slides/10008605.pdf)).

For independent researchers, the field now runs on **open, cheap infrastructure**. Gemma Scope 2 provides SAEs and transcoders for all 10 Gemma 3 models ([AF](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/YQro5LyYjDzZrBCdb/announcing-gemma-scope-2)). circuit-tracer v0.3.1 supports Gemma 3 through Neuronpedia ([Neuronpedia](https://www.neuronpedia.org/blog/assistant-axis)). Anthropic's released NLAs cover open models. Emergent-misalignment organisms reach down to 0.5B ([arXiv 2506.11613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11613)). Crosscoder code and models are open ([arXiv 2504.02922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02922v2)), the SPD repository accepts PRs ([GitHub](https://github.com/goodfire-ai/spd)), and Cywinski et al.'s secret-knowledge organisms are public. The track record backs this up. The ICLR 2026 paper "Emergent Misalignment is Easy, Narrow Misalignment is Hard" is a MATS project. It found a shared linear misalignment direction across fine-tunes, with 99%-coherent organisms ([ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/a79237d6c47feef24c1df723cb41f674-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The real change in 2026 is epistemic, not methodological. The field now has more ways to produce explanations (features, attribution graphs, natural-language verbalizations, oracle answers) than ways to check them. The labs' own limitation sections, GDM's WorkspaceBench and the agents' causal-control gap in SAEScientist-Bench all point to the same bottleneck. The scarce resource is **ground truth and causal validation**, not compute. That is why the window is unusually good for independent researchers: building a model organism with a planted, known mechanism, or running a careful causal test against a strong baseline, costs hundreds of dollars and directly targets what the frontier labs say they lack. The counter-view deserves weight. Ambitious reverse-engineering, through parameter decomposition, interference weights and sparse-by-design models, may yet turn out to be the only route to guarantees, and pragmatic probing may simply hit a ceiling on frontier behaviors. Toy-scale weight-level work is therefore a defensible contrarian bet, not a backwater.

### 1. Separate eval-awareness belief from surface cues

This problem is first because 2026 papers directly conflict and it is cheap to test. Probes reach AUROC ≥0.98 even when behavior drops below chance, single-layer steering does almost nothing, and the link to refusal is "correlative" ([arXiv 2606.23583](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23583v1); [arXiv 2608.27340](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27340v1)). GDM lists eval-awareness suppression as an area to empower ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MnkeepcGirnJn736j/how-can-interpretability-researchers-help-agi-go-well)), and Anthropic's NLAs found unverbalized eval awareness in Claude ([NLA](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/)). **First experiment:** build a 2x2 dataset that crosses eval-style formatting (multiple choice, "Question:" headers, benchmark-like phrasing) with real eval-versus-deployment content. Train probes on each axis separately on Qwen3-8B and test cross-transfer. Then apply multi-layer steering along each direction and measure the change in a downstream misbehavior rate, such as sandbagging or refusal. **Publishable partial result:** showing that the standard eval-awareness direction is mostly explained by format cues, or identifying a residual direction that both survives format controls and causally moves behavior. Either is a clean workshop paper that resolves part of a live disagreement. First read 2608.13329 and 2608.21766, whose findings the notes did not retrieve.

### 2. Measure where activation verbalizers confabulate

Anthropic itself states that NLAs confabulate specifics and add inferences not present in the activation ([NLA](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/nla/)). WorkspaceBench now exists to evaluate such tools, and Transluce is scaling Activation Oracles ([Transluce](https://transluce.org/news)). Because trained NLAs for open models were released, no RL training is needed. **First experiment:** take a released open-model NLA. Inject known concepts with steering vectors or plant facts with a small LoRA, so the ground truth is controlled. Then score the verbalizations on three counts: whether the theme is detected, whether specific details are correct, and how often they hallucinate content that was not injected. Compare against a linear-probe baseline and a logit-lens baseline on the same questions. **Publishable partial result:** a confabulation taxonomy with rates. For example: "themes are recovered X% of the time, but entity-level specifics are fabricated Y% of the time, and fabrication rises with layer depth." A cheap consistency check that flags confabulated outputs would make the paper stronger.

### 3. Approximate resampling-based step importance cheaply

GDM asks how to reverse-engineer computation spread over many resampled tokens and says it does not know ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MnkeepcGirnJn736j/how-can-interpretability-researchers-help-agi-go-well)). Thought Anchors treats the sentence as only "a reasonable starting point" as the unit of analysis ([arXiv 2506.19143](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19143v4)). **First experiment:** on R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B or 7B with MATH problems, compute ground-truth sentence importance by on-policy resampling with vLLM on one rented A100/H100. Then test cheap proxies against it: attention received by later tokens, activation-difference norms, probe-based answer-confidence shifts, and an attribution-graph score. Report rank correlations, and test alternative segmentation units such as clauses or "decision tokens". **Publishable partial result:** a proxy that reaches high rank correlation with resampling at 1–5% of the cost. A negative result showing that all cheap proxies fail on specific step types, such as uncertainty management, is also publishable.

### 4. Steer fine-tuning toward narrow rather than emergent misalignment

Emergent misalignment is linearly represented and shared across fine-tunes, and organisms exist down to 0.5B ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/a79237d6c47feef24c1df723cb41f674-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv 2506.11613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11613)). Whether training can be steered without changing the data or loss is listed as open ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MnkeepcGirnJn736j/how-can-interpretability-researchers-help-agi-go-well)). **First experiment:** run a head-to-head comparison of CAFT, preventative steering along the misalignment direction, and inoculation prompting on the released 0.5B–8B organisms. Measure three things: retention of the narrow task, the rate of broad misalignment, and how robust each intervention is as the number of training steps grows. **Publishable partial result:** a comparison table with a "breaking point" curve showing at how many gradient steps each method fails. Check the 2026 EM follow-ups (2604.28082, 2605.00842, 2606.20225) first to avoid duplication.

### 5. Recover a planted change by blind model diffing

L1 crosscoders misattribute shared concepts as unique to the fine-tuned model. BatchTopK plus Latent Scaling fixes this, but the fix was tested only on Gemma 2 2B ([arXiv 2504.02922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02922v2)). **First experiment:** have a collaborator, or a script that hides the choice from you, apply one of several known interventions to a 2B model: an EM fine-tune, refusal ablation, a synthetic-document fact implant, or inoculation. Then run BatchTopK crosscoders, the activation-difference lens and logit-diff amplification, and score whether each method identifies the planted change without being told. **Publishable partial result:** a small "diffing benchmark" with planted ground truth, and a ranking of methods on it. This fills the validation gap GDM names. Extending Latent Scaling to Gemma 3 or Qwen3 is a natural add-on.

### 6. Test whether SAEs earn their keep on unknown-discovery tasks

SAEs lose to probes on acting on known concepts ([arXiv 2502.16681](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16681v1)), but the case for using them to discover unknowns is still untested at scale ([arXiv 2506.23845](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23845)). **First experiment:** take secret-knowledge or hidden-goal organisms and give each method the same query budget: Gemma Scope 2 SAEs and transcoders, prompting, probing, model diffing, and, where possible, released NLAs. Score how often each recovers the hidden objective. **Publishable partial result:** a fair comparison across tools on an auditing proxy task. That is exactly the evaluation the "actionable" position paper calls for ([arXiv 2605.11161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11161)).

### 7. Stress-test the validity of deception and sandbagging probes

GDM rates deception probes as proof-of-concept and internals-based sandbagging detection as underdeveloped ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MnkeepcGirnJn736j/how-can-interpretability-researchers-help-agi-go-well)). An ICML 2026 MI Workshop talk showed that final-token safety probes fail ([ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/54071)). **First experiment:** train deception probes on an 8B model and vary three things: prompt template, token position (final versus mean-pooled versus attention-head probes), and distribution shift (role-play deception versus incentivized deception). **Publishable partial result:** a map of where probe accuracy collapses, plus one robustness fix, such as multi-position aggregation, that recovers most of the loss.

### 8. Measure how introspection scales in small open models

All of Anthropic's introspection and workspace evidence comes from Claude ([transformer-circuits.pub](https://transformer-circuits.pub/)). **First experiment:** replicate concept-injection introspection tests on Qwen3 and Gemma 3 from 1B to 9B parameters, and measure the detection rate against injection strength and layer. **Publishable partial result:** a scaling curve. If the effect is absent below some size, that negative result is informative in its own right, as the notes observe.

### 9. Cluster SPD's rank-one components into mechanisms

SPD finds only rank-one components per layer, needs better stability to scale, and lacks a principled measure of how simple a component is ([arXiv 2506.20790](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20790v1); [Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research/interpreting-lm-parameters)). **First experiment:** using the open repo on its toy models, cluster rank-one components across layers by their co-activation and attribution patterns. Validate the clusters against the mechanisms known by construction in the toy model. **Publishable partial result:** a clustering method that recovers the planted multi-layer mechanisms, contributed upstream as a PR. This is fully Mac-feasible.

### 10. Characterize interference weights in self-trained tiny models

Anthropic's July 2025 toy model and August 2026 one-layer LM study show that interference is still an active question ([paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/interference_effectiveness_helpfulness/index.html)). **First experiment:** train one- and two-layer transformers on a Mac. Replicate the interference-weight decomposition, then extend it to two layers, where attention composition creates new interference paths. **Publishable partial result:** a demonstration of how interference compounds or cancels across layers. This is the contrarian "ambitious interpretability" bet; it is lower in near-term safety payoff but uncrowded.

Problems such as latent-reasoning models (Coconut/CoDI) and whether synthetic-document-fine-tuned models really believe implanted facts are also listed as open by GDM. The notes give too little detail on the prior work to rank them confidently.
