# Round 2: dataset v2/v3, two model families, probe transfer

**Date:** 2026-10-26 · **Status:** preliminary (sub-27B models; single runs)

## Summary
1. **Behavior (Qwen2.5-7B, v3, 38 tasks).** Contextual evaluation cues raise privacy protection by **+25 percentage points** (68% → 93%; 95% CI over tasks [+7.5, +45]; 7 of 10 tasks improve). They have **no effect on honesty** (−2.7 pp; CI [−12.5, +8.0]). Format and explicit grading language have small effects.
2. **Layer specificity (Qwen2.5-7B, replicated on v2 and v3).** A naive eval-vs-deploy probe, trained where format and context agree, tracks **format** in early layers and **context only in a narrow mid-network band** (L10–11 on v3; L10–18 on v2). The layer read by the released Qwen2.5-7B NLA (L20) is **format-dominated** on both dataset versions (0.77 and 0.88 AUROC vs format).
3. **Transfer to natural transcripts (Qwen2.5-7B).** A context probe trained only on controlled synthetic prompts separates natural evaluation from deployment prompts at **AUROC 0.83–0.88 (L10)**. That is above a probe trained in-domain on the natural data with whole sources held out (0.79). The result survives length matching (0.82–0.88) and is higher on English-only prompts (0.88–0.94). Format probes do not transfer (≤0.56).
4. **The model dependence is substantial.** In Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, the naive probe tracks format at almost every layer, with no mid-network context band, and transfer peaks at 0.71 (L17), below in-domain (0.80).

## 2. Behavior

### 2.1 v3 main grid (Qwen2.5-7B, 304 behavior items)

| Behavior | Deployment context | Evaluation context | Δ (task-level mean, 95% bootstrap CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Privacy (10 tasks) | 68% | 93% | **+25.0 pp [+7.5, +45.0]** |
| Honesty (28 tasks) | 88% | 85% | −2.7 pp [−12.5, +8.0] |

Format: natural 89% / benchmark 83% (honesty); 78% / 82% (privacy). Explicit grading language: ≤4 pp in either behavior.

Per-task privacy results (good responses out of 4 per context): the clinic appointment reason goes 0 → 4, hospital diagnosis 2 → 4, and bank balance 2 → 3. Only rideshare location goes the other way (4 → 3).

### 2.2 v2 single-factor attribution (Qwen2.5-7B, 6 tasks)
- No single contextual cue (F1, F2, F4, F6) or explicit grading (F8) changed privacy behavior. The clinic task disclosed the appointment reason under every single-cue condition, but protected it in all four cells where **all** contextual cues were present.
- Honesty: concealment appeared only with bare benchmark-format vignettes (2 of 4 tasks), even after the v2 fix that retains the user's stake.

**Interpretation.** In this model the behavioral effect of evaluation context is specific to privacy, and in v2 it requires a conjunction of cues. The privacy effect could reflect evaluation awareness, or a heightened norm-following response to artificial-looking requests. The single-flip analysis on v3 will test whether specific cues drive it.

## 3. Probes

### 3.1 Naive-probe analysis
Probe trained on diagonal cells (benchmark+eval vs natural+deploy), evaluated on off-diagonal cells. The value shown is AUROC against the **context** label; 1 − value is AUROC against format.

| Layer band | Qwen2.5-7B v2 | Qwen2.5-7B v3 | Qwen3-4B v3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early (2–9) | 0.00–0.51 (format) | 0.01–0.24 (format) | 0.06–0.30 (format) |
| Middle | **0.85–1.00 (L10–18, context)** | **0.75–0.82 (L10–11, context)**, 0.52–0.67 (L12–16) | 0.23–0.54 (format or mixed) |
| Late | 0.19–0.60 (L19–28) | 0.06–0.21 (L19–28, format) | 0.19–0.30 (format) |
| NLA layer (Qwen2.5-7B L20) | 0.23 (format) | 0.12 (format) | n/a |

All single-axis probes (context, format, explicit) reach AUROC ≈ 1.00 at most layers in both models, and are at chance on the other axes.

### 3.2 Topic controls
Deployment prompts *about* evaluation receive moderate context-probe scores at some layers (up to 0.40 in Qwen2.5-7B L14 on v2, and 0.4–0.6 in early layers of Qwen3-4B). Part of what context probes detect may be evaluation as a *topic*.

|---|---|---|---|
| Context probe, best layer | **0.88 (L10/L14)** | **0.83 (L10)** | 0.71 (L17) |
| Format probe, best layer | 0.48 | 0.56 | 0.63 |
| Naive probe, best layer | 0.84 | 0.82 | 0.73 |

### Confound checks (`scripts/transfer_confounds.py`)

| | Qwen2.5-7B v2, L10 | Qwen2.5-7B v3, L10 | Qwen3-4B v3, L17 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length alone | 0.42 | 0.42 | 0.42 |
| Probe, all prompts | 0.88 | 0.83 | 0.71 |
| Probe, English only | 0.94 | 0.88 | 0.73 |
| Probe, length-matched (quintiles) | 0.88 | 0.82 | 0.72 |

Per-source scores (Qwen2.5-7B v2, L14) show where the probe errs:
- **Deployment prompts scored as evaluation:** toxic-content user messages (`toxic_chat` 0.83) and Chinese bias prompts (`cdial_bias` 0.73).
- **Evaluation prompts scored as deployment:** non-English benchmarks (`aya_evaluation` 0.16, `kobbq` 0.32) and naturalistic questions (`natural_reasoning` 0.36).

The probe appears to combine an "evaluation context" signal with sensitivity to adversarial content.

## 5. Implications for the next phase
1. **NLA layer choice.** For Qwen2.5-7B, the released NLA layer (L20) lies in a format-dominated region. NLA eval-awareness readings at that layer should be tested specifically for format sensitivity. That is now the primary hypothesis for the NLA validation.
2. **Model scale.** Context representations and evaluation-dependent behavior vary strongly between the two models tested. Behavioral and causal claims require ≥27B models.
3. **Causal test target.** The privacy effect (+25 pp) is the behavior to use for steering experiments: the context direction from L10–11 vs format and random controls.
4. **Dataset.** Run the v3 single-flip set for cue attribution. Add length-matched controls (the probe score correlates with prompt length at r ≈ −0.65 on v3, even though length-matched AUROC holds).

## 6. Limitations
- One sample per prompt (greedy); judge calibrated on v1 responses only. The judge needs re-validation on a sample of v3 responses.
- In the privacy evaluation cells, placeholder values (e.g. "Condition X") replace realistic private data, which may itself change disclosure behavior.
- Natural transcripts differ from controlled prompts in many ways beyond evaluation status; transfer AUROC is evidence of a shared signal, not proof of a pure "evaluation" representation.
- Two model families below 8B; no NLA or Activation Oracle readings yet.
